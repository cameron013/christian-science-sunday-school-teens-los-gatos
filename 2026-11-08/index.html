00|Lesson Summary

The main message was “God's children never fell. We stand under the first record, where man is made in God's image and it is very good.”

Story time: “Maid, Arise”

Our middle school theatre read the healing of Jairus' daughter (Luke 8). Everyone in the house “knew” she was dead, but Jesus answered, “Weep not; she is not dead, but sleepeth.” She was “about twelve years of age,” right about our students' age, so “Maid, arise” is for them too.

We explored the lesson through three questions:

  1. Who created who? Only one record has God doing the creating, and that's the one that tells the truth about you. “And God saw every thing that he had made, and, behold, it was very good.” (Genesis 1:31)
  2. Who's the imposter? The imposter isn't you. It's the voice that says you're less than God made you. Jesus spoke past it when he called the man “Son” and told him to be of good cheer before he ever stood up. “Son, be of good cheer; thy sins be forgiven thee.” (Matthew 9:2)
  3. What are we fixing? You can't fix what was never broken. You can only see it more clearly. “Mortals are not fallen children of God. They never had a perfect state of being, which may subsequently be regained.” (Science and Health 476:13-15)

Case study: “The starting spot”

We finished the case we started the week before. Jack, a twelve-year-old, gets passed over for the starting five, and his best friend Ben gets the spot. Jack claps on the outside, but he can't stop counting what Ben has that he doesn't. Together we worked through how Jack can pray about it and what he can actually do. We had great feedback from the students on the case study, so we'll be giving it more time in class this coming Sunday.

01|Reading for This Week

Defeating the tempter by Karen Neff (Christian Science Sentinel).

Feeling like you deserve a problem, or that you're not praying “right,” may just be the “tempter” trying to demoralize you. Please give it a read and think about where that “you deserve this” voice shows up for you. We'll take a couple of minutes on it at the start of class.

02|Coming Up This Sunday, 11/15: Mortals and Immortals

The Golden Text is “. . . this mortal must put on immortality.” (I Corinthians 15:53). Our theme is “Being clothed upon”: blind Bartimaeus “casting away his garment, rose, and came to Jesus” (Mark 10:50). Please read the lesson ahead if you can, and come ready to think about these three questions:

  1. What garment would you throw off? Think of an old nickname, a reputation, or an “I'm just not a ___ person” you'd be glad to leave by the road. “By putting 'off the old man with his deeds,' mortals 'put on immortality.'” (Science and Health 262:7)
  2. Who are we? What are we exactly? “There is but one creator and one creation. This creation consists of the unfolding of spiritual ideas and their identities, which are embraced in the infinite Mind and forever reflected.” (Science and Health 502:29)
  3. Why Father Mother God? Why isn't it just Father God? “In divine Science, we have not as much authority for considering God masculine, as we have for considering Him feminine, for Love imparts the clearest idea of Deity.” (Science and Health 517:8)

That class recap is now up: Mortals and Immortals: Off with the Old, On with the New.

Teaching notes

An outline of the three questions, story time, and the case study, with citations. Recap of class on November 8, 2026. No take-home worksheet was sent that week.

Three questions

1. Who created who? Only one record has God doing the creating, and that is the one that tells the truth about you. “And God saw every thing that he had made, and, behold, it was very good.” (Genesis 1:31)

2. Who's the imposter? The imposter isn't you. That voice says you're less than God made you. Jesus spoke past it when he called the man “Son” and told him to be of good cheer before he ever stood up. “Son, be of good cheer; thy sins be forgiven thee.” (Matthew 9:2)

3. What are we fixing? You can't fix what was never broken. You can only see it more clearly. “Mortals are not fallen children of God. They never had a perfect state of being, which may subsequently be regained.” (Science and Health 476:13-15)

Illustration: Maid, Arise
