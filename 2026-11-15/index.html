00|Lesson Summary

The main message was “Throwing away the old clothes represents coming full circle out of poverty, blindness, and a very limited perspective on life.”

We focused on the Bible story of Bartimaeus (Mark 10:46-52). He sat by the road in poverty and blindness, and in a very limited view of life. When Jesus called, “he, casting away his garment, rose, and came to Jesus” (Mark 10:50). He did not patch the cloak. He left it behind.

We also used the light spectrum analogy to add color to this story.

We used the illustration of the fish asking “What's water?” from David Foster Wallace's 2005 Kenyon College commencement speech, “This Is Water.” The line we sat with is:

We explored the lesson through two questions:

  1. What are we throwing off? Bartimaeus did not patch his cloak. He threw it off. Old labels, fears, and grudges were never part of who we are, so we do not have to hug our tatters close. “Let us disrobe error. Then, when the winds of God blow, we shall not hug our tatters close about us.” (Science and Health 201)
  2. What are we putting on? We are never left standing there with nothing. When the old garment goes, we are “clothed upon” with what God already gave us: light, life, and gladness. “For we that are in this tabernacle do groan, being burdened: not for that we would be unclothed, but clothed upon, that mortality might be swallowed up of life.” (II Corinthians 5:4)
01|Case Study

Case study: “The note that cracked”

We applied the case study about Emma, a junior-high trumpet player who cracked a high note at the spring concert. Emma is dealing with fear and being labeled. We also discussed the importance of writing as an exercise for us when writing a treatment. Applying the values from this week's lesson, we worked on this worksheet to treat our friend. Students are welcomed to complete and use it as an outline for further study and practice!

02|Reading for This Week

Feeling God’s love again by Solace Smith (Christian Science Sentinel, TeenConnect: Your Healings, October 12, 2026, Vol. 128, Issue 41).

A high schooler preparing a choir solo was afraid of missing the high notes. She prayed to see her singing as God’s expression and sang freely, like Emma in our case study.

04|Coming up this week: Soul and Body (Sun Nov 22)

Please read this week's Bible Lesson, Soul and Body, before Sunday. Here are the three questions we'll take up in class.

  1. Where are you retreating to? What are we running from, and what will we find when we get there? “Sooner or later we shall learn that the fetters of man's finite capacity are forged by the illusion that he lives in body instead of in Soul, in matter instead of in Spirit.” (Science and Health 223:3)
  2. Where are we racing to? This is not a race against anyone else. “let us lay aside every weight, and the sin which doth so easily beset us, and let us run with patience the race that is set before us,” (Hebrews 12:1)
  3. What are we reflecting? It is not what goes in that shapes us, but what comes out. “Not that which goeth into the mouth defileth a man; but that which cometh out of the mouth, this defileth a man.” (Matthew 15:11)
05|Talk about it at Dinner
One question a parent can ask.

What's one old label, fear, or habit you'd be glad to take off this week, and what would you want to put on in its place?

There is no right answer. The class talked about this as taking off old clothes and putting on new ones.
